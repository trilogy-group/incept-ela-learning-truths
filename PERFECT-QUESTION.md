# What a perfect question is

The universal criteria on record, 551 of them, by the part of a question or lesson they govern. Each links to its record, which says where it came from and what argues against it. A criterion marked contested or open is not settled. Criteria that hold only in one domain are on its page under [domains/](domains/); how to make questions that meet them is in [GENERATING.md](GENERATING.md).

## Stem: the question itself

- A lesson's driving question is open, never yes or no, answerable from the text alone, about what the text shows or how it shows it, and short. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#a-driving-question-the-text-can-answer)
- Write each stem as a full question that names everything it asks students to relate, so that a student who knows the material can answer it with the options covered. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#a-stem-that-stands-on-its-own)
- A writing task must say what to write about, in what form, at what length and from which sources, leaving the student nothing to guess. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#a-writing-task-leaves-nothing-to-guess)
- A cause question over a chain of causes must name the one link it asks about, because 'what causes this?' makes several links defensible. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#ask-about-one-link-in-a-chain)
- A question on a taught word must make the learner use the idea, never define the word in the question or key an answer that just repeats its definition. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#dont-define-the-word-being-tested)
- Build each part of an open question so that a vague, generic answer cannot earn credit and the part can be scored on its own. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#each-part-specific-scored-on-its-own)
- No task may require students to disclose their own relationships. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#never-require-disclosing-ones-own-relationships)
- Every premise a question's wording takes for granted must be true of the text, because a question can presuppose something the text contradicts. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#no-false-premise-in-the-question)
- Ask for one thing in each stem or part of a question: no compound verbs such as identify and describe, no second question, no joining of separate concepts. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#one-task-per-part)
- When a question asks about part of a picture or diagram, point to that part by name or highlight but never state the property the student must read from it. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#point-to-the-part-not-its-property)
- Write each question for its own text and teach no rule of thumb many texts break, such as checking the title or expecting the meaning or evidence in one sentence. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#questions-written-for-their-own-text)
- An argument task's prompt and sources must leave room for more than one defensible position. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#room-for-more-than-one-position)
- A multiple-select item says in its stem how many answers to choose, or to choose all that fit, and that number matches the key. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#say-how-many-answers-to-choose)
- Teach reading the question as its own step, marking the word that says what to do and the word naming the target, with the usual misreadings as wrong options. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#teach-reading-the-question)
- Make the task the most prominent thing on each screen, checked on the rendered screen, because a text-pattern check can pass a screen where supporting material buries the task. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#the-task-leads-every-screen)

## Options: the choices offered

- Choose how many options a question offers on purpose: avoid two-option items, cap how many a young reader must weigh, and never add a filler option to reach a count. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#choose-the-number-of-options-on-purpose)
- In a two-part item asking for evidence behind the first answer, only the keyed evidence may support the right answer, and each wrong evidence option should fit a wrong reading. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#each-evidence-option-fits-one-reading)
- When the evidence for an answer spans two sentences, make them one selectable unit, so the learner isn't asked to choose between two right answers. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#evidence-across-two-sentences-is-one-pick)
- List excerpts from a text in the order they occur in it, since a reordered list can make a line seem to belong to the wrong speaker or moment. [record](groups/the-text.md#keep-excerpts-in-the-texts-order)
- When words in a sentence become answer choices, blanks or glossary buttons, keep the sentence's punctuation attached to its word or final choice, and check wrapping before and after selection. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#keep-punctuation-with-its-sentence)
- No surface feature may single out the key: keep options alike in length, grammar, register and scope. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#no-cue-in-the-item-itself)
- When students pick evidence from a set, the strongest evidence must be in the set, and the rubric must not credit evidence they can't pick. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#offer-the-strongest-evidence)
- In a gap-fill choice, every option should come from the same category, read grammatically in the sentence and differ in meaning there, so only understanding the text decides. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#only-the-text-decides-the-blank)
- When a stem asks for two steps, options that all share the first step's outcome give it away; drop that step or let the options span its outcomes. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#options-must-not-give-a-step-away)
- Check that the options differ in meaning, not just in wording: identical or equivalent options leave more than one right answer and no real distractor. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#options-that-differ-in-meaning)
- In a select-all question every option must be decided by the text alone, and finding one right answer must not point to another. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#select-all-options-stand-alone)
- Make each selectable piece of text a whole word, phrase, clause or sentence, never a fragment cut mid-word or mid-phrase. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#selectable-text-comes-in-whole-pieces)

## Key: the right answer and its marking

- In a comparison item, the keyed explanation must name a cause that differs between the things compared; a cause that applies to both explains nothing about the difference. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#a-cause-that-explains-the-difference)
- Derive a text's theme from patterned details in that text, never from a moral the unit planned in advance. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#a-theme-drawn-from-the-text-itself)
- Every part of an item, shown to students or not, must be factually right and agree with the key, since a wrong key or fact anywhere fails the whole item. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#a-wrong-key-or-fact-fails-the-item)
- Accept every answer the text defensibly supports, and check that the hints and the reveal don't steer students toward only one of them. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#accept-every-answer-the-text-supports)
- Accept only the forms of a taught word that fit the blank's sentence, and meet a wrong form of it with a redirect that doesn't supply the right form. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#accept-only-the-word-form-that-fits)
- No correct answer in a set may contradict another, and what one step marks right or teaches must still hold in the next, so check the whole set. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#answers-in-a-set-must-agree)
- Where a highlighted span earns credit by how much of a keyed span it covers, cap its length, since a whole-passage highlight covers every key. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#cap-the-length-of-a-scored-highlight)
- On a retried choice item, cut credit with each wrong pick and give none once only the key is left, so trying every option earns nothing. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#credit-falls-with-each-wrong-pick)
- Say what an answer accepts in one place, since rules written separately for the same answer drift apart. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#define-what-counts-once)
- An item's parts must agree: the stem asks for what the options offer, the key names an option that exists, and the explanation and feedback support that key. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#every-part-of-an-item-agrees)
- In a question meant to show the text was understood, key the answer to a relationship the text asserts, since a single retrievable fact is easy to leak. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#key-to-a-relationship-the-text-asserts)
- A choice question needs one answer the text settles; check the right option, and each wrong one, on its own. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#one-answer-the-text-settles)
- Spread the right answer's position evenly across a set, and check it in code. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#spread-the-right-answers-position)
- Every claim in a keyed answer must trace to something the source says, but naming the category of what it describes is not an error. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#the-key-must-trace-to-the-source)

## Distractors: the wrong options

- A note shown beside a choice before the learner picks must give the reason someone might believe that choice, never argue against it, or it gives the answer away. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#a-choices-note-argues-for-it)
- In a word-meaning check, make every wrong option a word the lesson taught, because an untaught word can be ruled out by unfamiliarity alone and so tests nothing. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#distractors-from-the-words-taught)
- Build each wrong option from a different, named misconception or predictable error that students really make, so that choosing it shows what was misunderstood. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#each-wrong-option-a-named-misconception)
- In a select-all item, every wrong statement must contain an error that can be named, because a statement that is merely less good is not false. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#every-wrong-statement-names-its-error)
- Teach and test a word or concept with surface-different examples that share its one deep feature, and near-miss non-examples that each fail for a single reason the feedback names. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#near-misses-that-fail-for-one-reason)
- No wrong option may be the only one carrying the words the teaching just made prominent, because it pulls the learners who listened hardest toward the wrong answer. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#no-wrong-option-alone-carries-the-taught-words)
- Don't build a wrong option from a detail that is true in another version or retelling of the story, because it penalises the learner who reads widely. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#no-wrong-option-true-in-another-version)
- In an evidence hunt, label each phrase a student can pick as key clue, other clue, weaker detail or distractor, give each its own feedback, then ask which is strongest. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#rank-the-evidence-not-just-find-it)
- Have the worked example reject a likely error aloud, then offer that same error as a wrong option in the independent check, attempted cold with no hint first. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#the-rejected-error-returns-as-a-distractor)
- Each wrong option must attract a student who misunderstood, and only the tested knowledge or the text may rule it out, never common sense or surface cues. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#wrong-options-only-knowledge-can-reject)
- When an item tests combining features or sources, include a plausible wrong option right on only one, and key the item so only all of them together answer it. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#wrong-options-right-on-only-one-feature)
- Write each wrong option as a plain claim stated as if true, not as a report of what learners believe. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#wrong-options-stated-as-claims)
- Put in wrong options that a shortcut would choose, so that only applying the rule to the new material works. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#wrong-options-that-catch-the-shortcut)
- Make each wrong option stand for a kind of comprehension failure, such as paraphrase without inference or an unwarranted link, and route feedback by the kind chosen across items. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#wrong-options-that-diagnose-the-process)
- Use wrong options that are true elsewhere in the text, or were true earlier, but don't answer this question. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#wrong-options-true-elsewhere-in-the-text)
- In science content, make each wrong option wrong only as its named misconception is, never garbled some other way, since a garbled option teaches its garble to every reader. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#wrong-options-wrong-for-the-right-reason)

## Stimulus: the text, document or source a question rests on

- A source must be rich enough for its task, with a distinct part for each question and, when paired, distinct evidence; any false detail or failing question fails the set. [record](groups/the-text.md#a-source-rich-enough-for-its-task)
- When adapting a text for young readers, leave unsuitable material out by declared selection, never silent rewriting, and keep hard facts such as coercion or racism visible without endorsing them. [record](groups/the-text.md#adapt-by-declared-cuts-keep-hard-facts)
- Cap a passage sentence for young readers at about 35 words, and build complexity from subordinate clauses rather than from longer or rarer words. [record](groups/the-text.md#cap-passage-sentences-near-35-words)
- Choose each practice text after the skill it is for, so that the skill is the only way through it, never a text the learner already knows or can guess. [record](groups/the-text.md#choose-a-text-that-needs-the-skill)
- Show an older text's context note on the route learners actually take, before the passage, and leave the original text unaltered. [record](groups/the-text.md#context-on-the-way-into-the-passage)
- Label invented data as invented, keep its units and scale, tell counts, rates, percentages and indices apart, and never let a qualitative model become a number it can't support. [record](groups/the-text.md#data-keeps-its-units-kind-and-precision)
- A question asking for a conclusion from data or an experiment must give enough data to reach it, vary only the factor tested and never present correlation as cause. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#enough-data-for-the-conclusion-asked-for)
- When the skill is combining sources or formats, every source given must be needed to reach the answer. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#every-source-given-must-be-needed)
- Quote exactly, and check every quotation against the text in code. [record](groups/the-text.md#exact-quotations)
- Match how explicit and connected a text is to its readers' knowledge: highly cohesive texts help readers new to a topic but can lower deeper understanding for knowledgeable readers. [record](groups/the-text.md#fit-a-texts-cohesion-to-what-readers-know)
- Set a course's passage lengths, genre mix and demands from its own grade and test, building toward that test's reading load, rather than porting a sibling course's harder texts. [record](groups/the-text.md#fit-each-course-to-its-grade-and-test)
- Every document in a set bears on its question, together they cover the time and place it asks about, and their differing viewpoints support more than one defensible argument. [record](groups/the-text.md#fit-the-documents-to-the-question)
- When levelling a primary source for younger readers, keep its first-person voice, the property that justifies using it, so the firsthand and secondhand contrast survives. [record](groups/the-text.md#keep-a-levelled-sources-first-person-voice)
- When a story personifies a natural process, keep the informational text paired with it in plain mechanism language, and make the difference between them explicit. [record](groups/the-text.md#keep-the-science-plain-beside-a-personified-story)
- Keep the passage still while teaching appears around it, reserving the space from the start, because text that shifts when modelling begins is jarring. [record](groups/the-text.md#keep-the-text-still-as-teaching-appears)
- Show verse in its authored lines wherever it appears, in passages, pictures or films and at any screen width, never joined or re-wrapped, since its line breaks carry meaning. [record](groups/the-text.md#keep-verse-line-breaks-everywhere)
- Count a unit as building knowledge only when it teaches something about the world learners don't already know; familiar themes such as family or pets give practice, not knowledge. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#knowledge-units-teach-something-new)
- Make every source's label, which work, by whom, where, when and what kind of document, match the source text, in generated items and in the source bank. [record](groups/the-text.md#labels-that-match-their-sources)
- In history texts, show an achievement's limits and never play down the harm done to the people it affected. [record](groups/the-text.md#limits-and-harms-in-history-texts)
- When an adaptation changes a fact of the original, such as a count, record the change, keep it consistent everywhere, and never assess it as true of the original. [record](groups/the-text.md#log-an-adaptations-changed-facts)
- In guided practice, mark the key quote in place in the text rather than lifting it out, since extracting it does the locating step for the learner, out of sight. [record](groups/the-text.md#mark-the-evidence-where-it-sits)
- When assigning part of a longer text, mark where the part ends as well as where it starts, so the learner need not work out the end from paragraph numbers. [record](groups/the-text.md#mark-where-an-assigned-reading-ends)
- Never pass an invented text or source off as real: use real ones, copied word for word with checkable attribution, and plainly label anything constructed, adapted, retold or machine-written. [record](groups/the-text.md#never-present-an-invented-source-as-real)
- History and technology content avoids known framing traps, such as monolithic groups, events as destiny, contested readings as settled fact, one side without agency, lone-genius invention and unexamined sources. [record](groups/the-text.md#no-framing-traps-in-history-content)
- In science content, never present one factor as the sole driver or fix of a system with several causes; name the other causes and the uncertainty. [record](groups/the-text.md#no-single-cause-for-a-many-cause-system)
- Judge engagement separately for fiction and nonfiction, and give nonfiction its own hook rather than a lower bar, since readers' motivation for the two was not highly associated. [record](groups/the-text.md#nonfiction-needs-its-own-hook)
- Set the studied text in one typeface and all instructions, definitions and feedback in another, used the same way throughout, so learners can tell the text from the teaching. [record](groups/the-text.md#one-typeface-for-the-text-one-for-teaching)
- While students read, show only the text; bring questions only after the reading, take reading help away once a question appears, and let students look back at the text anytime. [record](groups/the-text.md#only-the-text-while-reading)
- Questions, hints, feedback and rubrics may use only the student's version of the text so far; a word asked about must be in it, exactly or as a family form. [record](groups/the-text.md#only-what-the-student-has-read)
- Pair two texts only for a named comparison or synthesis purpose, not merely to set them side by side. [record](groups/the-text.md#pair-texts-only-for-a-named-purpose)
- Every reread sends the student back to a chosen part of the text for a purpose they are told. [record](groups/the-text.md#rereads-with-a-stated-purpose)
- Put every text a learner must read in the lesson's main path, never only behind a control they must notice, and show any text the passage alludes to. [record](groups/the-text.md#show-every-text-the-lesson-points-to)
- Give students the source itself to analyse, the excerpt, map, picture or chart the item says it rests on, never a prose description, summary or note about it. [record](groups/the-text.md#show-the-source-dont-describe-it)
- Whenever a task, prompt or feedback asks a student to find or cite evidence or sends them back to the text, keep the text on screen. [record](groups/the-text.md#show-the-text-the-task-needs)
- In a play's text on screen, show a speaker's name only where a speech starts, as a printed script does, not on every line. [record](groups/the-text.md#speaker-names-only-where-speeches-start)
- When a text is released in stages, add each part to one continuous column, and keep everything already unlocked there to reread and its audio to replay. [record](groups/the-text.md#staged-text-never-locks-behind-the-reader)
- Write teaching fiction as a real story, with a want, an obstacle and a turn shown in scenes, and let its theme be acted out rather than stated. [record](groups/the-text.md#teaching-fiction-must-be-a-real-story)
- Keep every passage students must read suited to their age and within, or only just above, their grade's reading band. [record](groups/the-text.md#text-within-the-grades-reading-band)
- Choose a course's texts for their purpose, the knowledge they build in sequence, a balance of genres and their quality, judged by reading them, not by a readability score alone. [record](groups/the-text.md#texts-chosen-for-more-than-readability)
- Choose texts whose names, characters and cultures let learners see themselves, since culturally familiar material is read faster, recalled more accurately and understood with fewer errors. [record](groups/the-text.md#texts-in-which-learners-see-themselves)
- Give a reading test whole, coherent passages rather than simplified summaries, and serve all the items on one passage together. [record](groups/the-text.md#whole-passages-with-their-items-together)
- To ease a hard passage written for learners, write it closer to how people talk, packing fewer idea units into each clause. [record](groups/the-text.md#write-a-hard-passage-closer-to-speech)
- A text read aloud in full may sit above the learners' reading level, up to what they understand by listening, so decoding doesn't cap what they learn. (contested) [record](groups/the-text.md#above-level-text-when-read-aloud)

## Scoring guide: the rubric or marking guide for written answers

- A marking guide describes the reasoning that earns credit in a few checks, not facts or keywords, which can be wrong or matched by an answer lacking the reasoning. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#guides-describe-reasoning-not-fact-lists)
- When learners judge which staging of a text convinces them, accept either choice but credit only a reason tied to the text's words, not lighting or camera alone. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#judge-a-staging-by-the-texts-words)
- Mark a prediction on whether the learner used the idea, not on whether it comes true. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#mark-the-reasoning-not-the-outcome)
- When a part has one intended answer, its scoring guide accepts other wordings of that answer, not related ideas or a broader or narrower category. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#one-answer-many-wordings)
- Give each point in a mark scheme to a discrete element that can be checked on its own, never award an element twice, and list acceptable equivalent answers in advance. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#one-point-for-each-checkable-element)
- Score the working separately from the final answer and let later parts credit work that follows from the student's own earlier answer, so a slip costs only one point. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#one-slip-costs-one-point)
- In a task built on given sources, the marking guide's examples of outside knowledge must not repeat what the sources already say. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#outside-knowledge-must-come-from-outside)
- Make marking require the content the task exists for, so an answer with that content wrong can't pass on strong language, structure or evidence. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#require-the-criterion-the-task-is-for)
- A scoring rubric needs levels a marker can tell apart, every level reachable from the question, and less credit for a plausible misreading than for a correct reading. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#rubric-levels-a-marker-can-tell-apart)
- Make sure every part a question asks for appears in its sample answers, not only in the parts the student sees. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#sample-answers-cover-every-part)
- Write marking criteria for each question that say exactly what earns each point, not a generic category or task-type rubric, so vague answers fail and valid wordings pass. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#say-what-earns-each-point)

## Feedback: what a student is told after answering

- Make an on-screen guide an encouraging mentor more expert than the learner, not a peer, shown only at a few key moments and, for teenagers, never cartoonish or over-praising. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#a-guide-who-mentors-at-set-moments)
- Plan the follow-up for each kind of answer in advance: challenge a full answer further, hold level on a brief one, and step support down for the rest. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#a-planned-follow-up-for-every-answer)
- Make every reply depend on what the learner actually said, have each hint add what the answer lacks, and test that every branch, including 'I don't know', can fire. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#a-reply-that-depends-on-the-answer)
- When the text supplies a stronger word than an answer uses, treat the answer as unfinished, not wrong: accept it, then push once for the text's word. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#a-vocabulary-floor)
- Answer a prompt that has no right answer with an acknowledgement of what the learner said: nothing prewritten, which can give away what comes, and no correction or next step. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#acknowledge-where-there-is-no-right-answer)
- When a spoken word isn't accepted, show what was heard, the printed word and its say-it spelling, and offer, never force, its sound, since writing alone can't teach pronunciation. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#answer-a-missed-word-with-its-sound)
- When a learner gives a position without a reason, ask why before arguing against it. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#ask-why-before-testing-a-bare-stance)
- On a wrong choice, ask the learner why that option is not right before revealing the answer, so a retry becomes a thinking move instead of another guess. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#ask-why-the-wrong-choice-is-wrong)
- Correct an error immediately and briefly, test it, give other items, then retest it later. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#correct-briefly-retest-later)
- Follow a plausible wrong option with corrective feedback or a correction after the test, because a lure left uncorrected can plant false knowledge. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#correct-what-a-lure-might-teach)
- Open critical feedback on a learner's work with a fixed line saying the standard is high and they can reach it, then give specific critique, not praise padding. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#critique-that-states-high-standards)
- Give every open-ended task or exchange a defined end, such as a completed paragraph or a sign the idea is understood, so it doesn't run until someone gives up. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#every-exchange-needs-a-defined-end)
- Show the complete authored answer on every way out of an open question, including a pass, the last miss and a scoring outage, since passing answers are often partial. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#every-way-out-shows-the-whole-answer)
- Explanations, hints and feedback must be true of the item as given. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#explanations-must-fit-the-item-given)
- Give each wrong option its own feedback that names the error and likely misconception it stands for and says what to look at next. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#feedback-for-each-wrong-option)
- Make feedback and a guide's speech look unlike controls, keeping button styles for what acts when pressed, since learners may try to tap anything drawn like a button. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#feedback-must-not-look-like-a-button)
- Feedback on a wrong answer says where to look, never what the student has to produce, and only what is true of what they were given. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#feedback-that-sends-the-student-back)
- Show feedback directly under the answer or option it responds to, and keep the text in view whenever feedback says to look back. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#feedback-where-the-student-is-looking)
- When one answer leads straight into a linked one, hold feedback on the first until the second is given, because a verdict on the first can answer the second. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#hold-feedback-until-the-linked-answer)
- Put every part of a lesson's key idea where every learner meets it, such as the text, the narration or the closing screen, not only in replies to strong answers. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#land-the-key-idea-for-every-learner)
- Never refer to an option by its letter or position in feedback, hints, explanations or instructions; name it by what it says, since the display may reorder options. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#name-an-option-by-its-words)
- When an answer is partly right, say which part is right and ask only for the missing part. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#name-the-right-part-ask-for-the-rest)
- When feedback on writing finds several gaps, give one next step, on the earliest missing part in the order the writing builds, never a list or a chained second step. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#one-next-step-never-a-list)
- In a debate, push back once on the side the student took, only when the judge is sure of it, then offer a position they could defend, and mark nothing. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#one-push-back-in-a-debate)
- Give feedback at once on simple facts, and on concepts explain why an answer is right or wrong. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#quick-for-facts-explained-for-concepts)
- Before reading, meet a wrong prior-knowledge answer with 'find a connection, then let's go back and look', never 'that's wrong', and judge only that the answer is real. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#redirect-a-wrong-prior-knowledge-answer)
- For an interpretive question, show a model answer only after a committed attempt, labelled as one strong reading with an invitation to disagree, never as the answer. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#reveal-one-strong-reading-not-the-answer)
- When a wrong answer comes from a kind of thinking, give a transferable rule that separates right from wrong; point back to the text only for a missed fact. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#rules-for-thinking-errors-pointers-for-facts)
- Tell a student what a later answer adds to an earlier one only when the judge is sure, in a fixed authored line, never as a mark. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#say-what-the-second-answer-adds)
- A retry or hint message shown for every wrong answer must be true of every answer it can be shown for, and must not assume how another part was answered. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#shared-feedback-fits-every-answer)
- Keep corrective feedback short and aimed at the error; a correction that needs more is new teaching, so fix the item or its placement instead. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#short-feedback-or-fix-the-item)
- Show how other learners answered only after a learner has committed their own answer, and never with names. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#show-peers-answers-only-after-committing)
- Show a visible waiting state whenever the system is working on a learner's answer, so a pause reads as thinking rather than broken. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#show-that-the-system-is-working)
- When a question about a text is answered right or its answer is given, mark the lines in the text that prove the answer and bring them into view. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#show-the-lines-that-prove-the-answer)
- With adolescents, don't re-explain what a learner has shown they can do, and offer re-teaching rather than push it, since repeating what teens think they know feels infantilizing. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#teenagers-opt-in-to-re-teaching)
- After three misses in a row, a lesson still gives useful correction and a way to recover, never just an answer to copy or a dead end. (contested) [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#a-way-back-after-three-misses)
- Give every item an explanation that shows why its key is right and why each wrong option is wrong, rather than restating the answer. (contested) [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#explain-the-key-and-every-wrong-option)
- After a wrong answer, keep the attempt visible, diagnose its misconception, and pass only the learner's own fresh correction, never an auto-filled answer or a repeated stale selection. (contested) [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#keep-the-attempt-require-a-fresh-correction)
- Praise and encouragement name the specific process or strategy the learner used, never the person, stay truthful and never slip in the answer. (contested) [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#praise-the-process-never-the-person)

## Hints: help before the answer is given

- In independent practice, let students open a recap of what the worked example did at each step, drawn from the example's own material rather than the current answer. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#a-recap-of-the-worked-example)
- When a reader can decode a word but doesn't know its meaning, teach a routine: mark it, reread its sentence, reread around it, break it into parts. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#a-routine-for-a-words-meaning)
- Stop a cue inviting a first tap once the student has tapped that surface, and never restart it after a miss, where it reads as 'tap me again'. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#an-invitation-cue-ends-at-first-tap)
- Each rung of a hint ladder must add information and lead toward what the marking asks for. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#every-hint-rung-adds-something)
- Through a hint sequence, keep every earlier answer and hint on screen, since a hint that builds on the last answer is useless once that answer is gone. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#every-turn-of-a-hint-ladder-stays-visible)
- Fit a recall hint to the kind of miss, naming a near spelling or another taught word rather than just saying wrong, and give the word on the second miss. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#fit-the-hint-to-the-kind-of-miss)
- Offer help only after the student has tried, and order hints from general cues to specific ones, naming the clue's own words only in the last hint. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#help-after-a-try-cues-first)
- Help may paraphrase a hard line, say who speaks to whom or ask planned questions that draw out reasoning, but the interpretation, evidence and answer stay the learner's. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#help-may-paraphrase-not-interpret)
- Keep help quiet and optional: glosses, notes and a few named helpers open only when the learner asks or with feedback, never as a constant guide or per-part buttons. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#help-that-waits-to-be-asked)
- Advance a hint ladder only when the answer has changed since the last try, so resubmitting the same answer cannot unlock more help. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#hints-advance-only-on-a-changed-answer)
- In independent practice, leave the search to the learner: don't pre-mark candidates, name where to look, or say how many to find in an open hunt. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#leave-the-hunt-to-the-learner)
- Reveal an 'I'm not sure' route to help after a learner has been stuck a while, because shown up front the choice that can't be wrong becomes the cheap path. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#reveal-the-unsure-route-after-a-delay)
- Bring each support, such as a word bank, hints or sentence starters, onto the screen only at the step that uses it, so no screen opens on many live targets. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#stage-supports-dont-stack-them)
- After repeated misses on a search or a used-up hint ladder, offer help through one button the student must tap to accept, and never pick the answer unasked. (contested) [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#offer-help-never-pick-the-answer)

## Format: the kind of task

- Don't build help or independent work on a text as a chat thread: independent practice should produce the learner's own writing in a structured frame, not conversation turns. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#a-guide-is-not-a-chatbot)
- A performance task asks for a delivery choice, such as a stressed word or a pause, justified by the text's words, and accepts any choice the explanation supports. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#a-performance-task-needs-an-evidenced-choice)
- A test standing in for another must reproduce its interactions, navigation rules and per-sitting format mix, since a different interface changes how students respond and what scores mean. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#a-stand-in-test-matches-its-targets-interactions)
- Put a summary gap-fill's blanks on the causal links that hold the whole text together, never on incidental details or connecting words. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#a-summary-cloze-on-the-causal-links)
- Give learners a graphic organiser matched to the text's structure, such as a Venn diagram for comparison or a timeline for sequence, and reuse it for their speaking and writing. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#an-organiser-that-matches-the-text)
- Assess with more than a few kinds of task, because what a test asks shapes what is taught and one usual format can narrow teaching to it. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#assess-with-more-than-a-few-task-types)
- Label a task that rehearses part of an exam format as a component, and give the format's own name only to items that meet its full current specification. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#call-a-partial-task-a-component)
- Fit each item's format to its target and learners: production wherever the target is to produce, and the formats of the test the course prepares for that suit their grade. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#choose-the-format-the-target-needs)
- Teach a relationship by presenting two cases together with an explicit prompt to compare them; two cases met in turn without the prompt work no better than one. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#compare-two-cases-side-by-side)
- Where there is time, explain through a dialogue that answers a learner's questions, or by refuting the wrong idea, rather than by straight exposition. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#dialogue-and-refutation-beat-exposition)
- Give each AI voice in a multi-voice discussion a distinct role and put one moderator in charge of turn order, since extra voices helped only once their roles differed. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#distinct-ai-roles-one-moderator)
- End each reading with a task that does something with the meaning, such as applying it, critiquing it or taking a position, not only with answering items. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#end-each-reading-by-using-its-meaning)
- Never ask a question or invite a response, on screen or aloud, without a way for the learner to give it, including a lesson's closing question. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#every-question-needs-a-way-to-answer)
- For an answer that needs evidence, have the learner first find and mark the evidence in the text, then explain it in their own words in a separate slot. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#find-the-evidence-first-then-explain)
- Use a question format only once the student's own player has been seen to render it, and keep a fallback format for the rest. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#formats-the-player-can-deliver)
- A frame for using a taught word includes the word and ends in 'because' or 'so that', never a blank for the word or its definition. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#frames-end-in-because-or-so-that)
- Keep true/false and other two-option items out of scored decisions, and score a multi-select item only when its stem says how many to choose and its scoring rule is declared. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#keep-guessable-formats-out-of-scoring)
- Keep item formats that leak as a family out of every mastery gate, serving them, if at all, only in a practice lane that never gates progress. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#leaky-formats-stay-out-of-mastery-gates)
- Make each evidence tap or highlight a decision that can be wrong and is then discussed, steered to the parts that matter, since untrained highlighting is rated low utility. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#make-each-evidence-mark-a-decision)
- Match an item's interaction to the shape of its answer, letting learners move what they must order. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#match-the-gesture-to-the-answer)
- Ask learners to connect an idea to another text only when the question can name a text they have read; otherwise ask for a prediction framed by the idea. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#name-the-text-to-connect-to)
- Put a clock on a test only when working against time is part of what it measures; on an untimed test a clock changes what is measured. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#no-clock-unless-speed-counts)
- Give each screen one forward button, and never show two buttons that do the same job. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#no-two-buttons-for-one-job)
- Never ask a question a student can answer yes or no or by a two-way pick: ask what or how, and give choice questions several plausible options. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#no-yes-or-no-questions)
- An ordering item needs one defensible order: use only elements with a definite place, such as dated events or discrete steps, never a gradual process or ongoing condition. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#one-defensible-order)
- Give every lesson in a course the same controls and components, so lessons differ in content and structure, not in how they work. [record](groups/making-and-reviewing.md#one-interface-across-lessons)
- Ask one thing at a time: one question per screen, and a two-part task as two turns, so the feedback can answer each part. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#one-question-at-a-time)
- In a why-question's answer frame, print the character's action already, leaving only the reason to write, so the answer cannot just restate the action. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#pre-fill-the-action-in-a-motive-frame)
- Choose the response type a skill needs (identify, complete or generate) before counting responses, and accept the pace that type allows rather than chasing a high count. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#response-type-before-response-count)
- Run a conversational check as a fixed script, with set prompts, a capped number of follow-ups and declared supports, because free chat can lead the witness. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#script-the-conversational-check)
- Introduce related new words as the structure they form, such as a web showing the concept and how the rest relate to it, not as a deck of equal cards. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#show-how-taught-words-relate)
- Include some items with more than one right answer, such as choose-two items, so learners don't come to expect exactly one right answer to every question. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#some-items-with-two-right-answers)
- Structure discussion: give thinking or writing time before calling on anyone, prefer a scripted argue-both-sides sequence to open debate, and have each student produce the final synthesis alone. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#structured-talk-then-a-synthesis-alone)
- A mastery test should use the formats students met during instruction. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#test-in-the-formats-taught)
- Give each interactive move in a course its own action, and never run three moves of one kind in a row, so the moves don't feel repetitive. [record](groups/making-and-reviewing.md#vary-the-learners-moves)
- Make each question in a set and each part of a multi-part item answerable from the source and scorable alone, with none restating, revealing or depending on another's answer. (contested) [record](groups/writing-questions.md#each-part-stands-on-its-own)
- Give a matching item more targets than prompts, without announcing which one is spare, so its last pairs can't be won by elimination. (contested) [record](groups/writing-questions.md#give-a-matching-task-a-spare-target)

## Demand: the thinking a task requires

- Start a modelled think-aloud that chains procedures by noticing something in the text and wondering about it, then work the procedures to answer that self-posed question. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#a-chained-model-starts-from-noticing)
- Treat a chart or graphic organiser as scaffolding, not the learning: have the learner compose the sentence the lesson builds toward, and cut the chart first when time runs short. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#a-chart-is-scaffolding-not-the-learning)
- A response frame may show an answer's shape but must never prefill the evidence or quotation, or supply the reasoning, the learner is meant to produce. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#a-frame-shows-the-shape-not-the-answer)
- An inference question's answer must be supported by the text but not stated in it, because a stated conclusion only tests finding. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#an-inference-is-never-written-out)
- Plan at least two inference points in every passage and ask a question that needs each one; an inference the text sets up that no question asks is decoration. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#ask-about-every-inference-the-text-sets-up)
- Ask inference questions about characters' goals, motives and relationships: they draw out the causal inferences skilled readers make, building story understanding as effectively as a text stating those links. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#ask-about-goals-and-motives)
- Ask only for operations with a purpose in the scenario, since an arbitrary instruction tests whether students follow directions, not the skill. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#ask-for-operations-with-a-purpose)
- For young learners, ask for the target skill directly, and give prerequisite results or split the item into steps rather than packing a chain of sub-problems into one stem. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#ask-young-learners-for-the-target-skill)
- Have learners collect more evidence than they will use and write about only the strongest of it. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#collect-widely-write-about-the-best)
- For learners who can't yet decode, spend reading time on decoding and fluency and build comprehension by listening, since reading comprehension is decoding times language comprehension. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#decoding-first-comprehension-by-listening)
- A task's thinking demand is the operation a learner must perform to answer it, not its stem's verb, the skill it claims, its difficulty label or expected pass rate. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#demand-is-what-the-student-does)
- Across a lesson, the questions on one source should range from easier to harder without strict ordering, while an exam-style set on one source keeps them at one level. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#difficulty-spreads-in-lessons-not-exam-sets)
- An evidence or written task must not be passable by copying one sentence from the source. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#dont-stop-at-one-copied-quote)
- Teach and credit each reasoning skill by its element: one dimension evidenced on both sides for comparison, a mechanism for cause, a baseline for change, a qualifying move for complexity. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#each-reasoning-skill-has-its-element)
- In an evidence task, have students find the part of the text where the evidence lives before they hunt for the exact line. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#find-the-part-then-the-line)
- Put an item's or a level's difficulty in the knowledge or reasoning it demands, never in labels, untaught words, hard syntax, negatives, obscure facts, padded reading or reworded easier lessons. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#hard-for-the-right-reason)
- Count a question as higher-order, such as application or analysis, only when it presents material new to the learner, since a longer stem over familiar content is still recall. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#higher-order-needs-material-not-seen-before)
- An inference, modelled or asked for, may combine only what the text shows with what every student can be assumed to know, never an outside cultural or world fact. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#inferences-rest-on-the-text)
- For learners who are behind, keep the task's demand and add support, such as guided reading, vocabulary support and targeted practice, instead of giving easier material. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#keep-the-demand-add-support)
- In a worked example, model every step up to the final thinking move and leave that move to the student, with the modelled steps still in view. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#leave-the-last-move-to-the-student)
- Limit what each step asks a reader to hold: few new names per section, few short options per item, no long reading without a check, each task on one screen. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#limit-the-load-of-each-step)
- Until a skill has been modelled, checks on the reading ask only what the text states; independent use of the skill comes after the model. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#literal-checks-until-the-skill-is-modelled)
- Judge a question set as a whole: it mixes kinds of demand, such as literal, inferential and evaluative, and no one kind dominates. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#mix-the-kinds-of-demand-in-a-set)
- In a modelled reading, show only actions a real reader takes on a text, such as rereading or highlighting, never crossing out or otherwise altering the passage. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#model-only-what-real-readers-do)
- When the words a lesson tells learners to search for differ from the text's own wording, model the link between them before asking learners to search. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#model-the-bridge-to-the-texts-wording)
- A worked example shows the reasoning, not just the answer: it thinks aloud where to look and why each tempting wrong choice is rejected, never making the thinking look easy. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#model-the-thinking-not-the-answer)
- In a modelled evidence hunt, never reject a line that is true evidence of something nearby: say what it shows and why the question asks for something else. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#no-false-rejections-in-a-modelled-hunt)
- Give each screen one primary thing to attend to. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#one-focus-per-screen)
- Plan content knowledge and the named reasoning operations applied to it as two progressions that lessons join: content alone is exposure, and a content-free skill is a generic worksheet. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#pair-knowledge-with-a-reasoning-operation)
- Raise a course's level by handing students more of the thinking, such as framing questions, choosing evidence, handling ambiguity and repairing their own reasoning, not by harder vocabulary alone. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#raise-the-level-by-handing-over-thinking)
- Teach and test the combined use of skills through whole tasks that truly need them together, defined by exemplars and a rubric, because passing every part doesn't show the whole. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#tasks-that-need-the-skills-together)
- Have students commit to one of two plausible explanations, predict what the next passage will show, then read it and revise their account. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#test-a-reading-against-the-next-passage)
- A check on a taught word must put it in a new situation, because a key that repeats the teaching example can be picked from memory without knowing the meaning. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#test-a-taught-word-somewhere-new)
- In a modelled reading, the conclusion must emerge from the quoted lines step by step and follow from them, never be reached first and decorated with quotes. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#the-conclusion-comes-out-of-the-quotes)
- Fit the share of inference questions to what the text affords: a very easy text supports few honest inferences, and a flat target manufactures forced or unsupported items. (contested) [record](groups/writing-questions.md#inference-share-follows-the-text)

## Alignment: fit to the objective, unit and what was taught

- Choose a lesson's target idea so that it can't be stated correctly without reading the text, since a takeaway every learner already knows makes the whole lesson blind-solvable. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#a-lesson-target-only-the-text-supplies)
- Before students start, give them one purpose for reading that points at the evidence the later task will use, and keep the lesson's question and steps on screen. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#a-reason-to-read-kept-in-view)
- A question asked before reading helps learners learn what it asks about and little else, so aim it at the lesson's most important goal. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#aim-a-prequestion-at-the-main-goal)
- Fit an item and its sources to the exact target and focus its standard or objective names, not to its broad topic, period, theme or kind of evidence. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#aligned-to-the-target-not-the-topic)
- Ask a question right after the part that answers it, make sure it needs that part, and never stop mid-sentence. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#ask-where-the-answer-was-given)
- An item, its options, hints and pointers may require only what the student has met by then on every path, common knowledge, or what the item or reference materials supply. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#assume-and-test-only-what-was-taught)
- Back every claim a lesson makes about a category with an example the students actually meet in it. [record](groups/the-text.md#back-each-category-claim-with-an-example)
- Aim each check at what the page is for: the detail the lesson's idea or theme turns on, never an incidental detail nothing depends on. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#check-the-detail-the-idea-turns-on)
- Keep every fact in an item, its sample answers and marking notes included, inside the period and region the request names, and check dates in code. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#keep-facts-inside-the-period-asked)
- Leave out anything the lesson does not teach or use, however interesting or familiar, above all at the start: irrelevant material lowers recall and transfer. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#leave-out-what-the-lesson-doesnt-teach)
- A course for one level must differ from the course for the level beside it in more than its lesson list, difficulty and units, because near-copies share their weaknesses. [record](groups/making-and-reviewing.md#neighbouring-courses-must-really-differ)
- Give each short teaching piece exactly one idea: something that can be stated as a single testable sentence. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#one-idea-per-teaching-piece)
- Teach at most one well-defined new skill or named procedure per lesson, remove anything that doesn't serve it, and let its review steps use only skills already taught. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#one-skill-per-lesson)
- Every hint, recap or feedback line that points to another step must name a step that exists, by its current name, and say what that step actually did. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#point-only-at-steps-that-exist)
- An item that needs content the stated assessment boundaries exclude is out of scope even if it is correct, and only the stated boundaries count as exclusions. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#stay-inside-the-assessment-boundaries)
- Model, practise and test the same thinking step, so every skill a gate tests has been practised before the gate. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#teach-and-test-the-same-thinking)
- The skill a lesson or item claims must be one its text affords and its task really demands at the stated depth, not recall or a neighbouring skill. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#test-the-skill-it-claims)
- The task verb, the skill an item claims and what its rubric rewards must match, so the instruction a student sees and the rubric ask for the same things. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#the-task-verb-matches-what-is-marked)
- Two blocks of one kind in a lesson must test different things; tell the writer what the earlier one covers. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#two-of-a-kind-must-differ)
- Should a check inside a film made from a text test the text itself, or may it test what the film argued? (open) [record](groups/not-settled.md#what-a-films-own-checks-should-test)

## Sequence: order and spacing across a lesson or course

- Teach a small fixed set of named thinking procedures that covers every skill the course models, with identical steps each time, and say the procedure's name whenever it is modelled. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#a-few-named-procedures-the-same-steps)
- Claim that a course builds knowledge only where a later activity needs what an earlier part added, so that taking the earlier part out would break it. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#a-later-lesson-must-need-the-earlier-one)
- A course whose learners climb stages needs a way to decide where each starts, or every learner defaults to the lowest stage and a stage ceiling never does any work. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#a-staged-course-needs-a-placement-step)
- Give learners who already read well above a course's band a test-out or measure-only path, instead of holding them on material they have outgrown. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#a-way-out-for-readers-above-the-band)
- Ask the lesson's question at the start and again at the end, keeping both answers side by side and marking neither. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#ask-the-lessons-question-first-and-last)
- Assign a whole text part by part from the lessons that use it, since a text left in a reader isn't delivered and opening it isn't reading it. [record](groups/the-text.md#assign-a-whole-text-from-its-lessons)
- Assume only what every path guarantees: each branch teaches what later content uses and agrees on facts, and topics another course teaches work in either order, from a new angle. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#assume-only-what-every-path-guarantees)
- A lesson may assume only what earlier lessons taught and the learner still holds fluently, so record what each lesson assumes and adds, and check the whole sequence in code. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#assume-only-what-was-taught-and-kept)
- Give background at the line that needs it. [record](groups/the-text.md#background-at-the-line-that-needs-it)
- Sequence the texts learners study so taught knowledge recurs and builds, and base every scored test and transfer item on unseen, unrelated texts whose answers were never taught. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#coherent-texts-to-learn-cold-texts-to-test)
- Before asking students to compare two texts, give them a single text that makes the comparison itself. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#compare-within-one-text-first)
- When one procedure is used as a tool inside another's step, condense it to one sentence, and run it in full only when it is the procedure being modelled. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#condense-a-procedure-used-as-a-tool)
- When a page repeats word for word a text the learner just finished reading, count that reading toward the page's reading hold rather than impose a second wait. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#count-a-reading-already-done)
- Run drama-based literacy work as a sustained, guided sequence over several hours rather than a single task, since its measured effects are larger that way. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#drama-work-as-a-sustained-sequence)
- Each text in a unit should need only knowledge an earlier text added or the unit states as entry knowledge. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#each-text-builds-on-the-last)
- Make every small task visibly add to one artifact the learner is building, such as a paragraph that fills as they work, so no step feels like a separate task. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#every-task-adds-to-one-visible-piece)
- Explain how a course works once, at first open, with a help entry to return to it, instead of re-explaining the machinery at the start of every unit. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#explain-the-course-once)
- Fade guidance for each skill across a unit as learners gain expertise, because heavy guidance helps novices but slows learners who already know the material. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#fade-guidance-as-expertise-grows)
- Keep questions during reading few and at natural breaks, because frequent ones can cut young readers' recall of what they don't ask about. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#few-questions-during-reading)
- Give every lesson the same parts in the same order, and change how much time each part gets rather than whether it appears. [record](groups/making-and-reviewing.md#fixed-lesson-parts-flexible-time)
- Keep earlier knowledge alive by making later lessons need it, and send to separate spaced review only what nothing later can honestly use. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#keep-knowledge-warm-by-using-it)
- Keep sounding a word out and splitting it into meaningful parts as separate steps: one teaches how the word is said, the other what its parts mean. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#keep-sounding-out-apart-from-word-parts)
- Build the topic knowledge a hard text assumes, and give it before the reading, not after. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#knowledge-before-the-text)
- Never gate a check on untaught content: a pretest often helps even when most answers are wrong, but only if learners meet the right answers afterwards. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#leave-a-pretest-on-new-content-ungated)
- Plan many meaningful, spaced encounters with each taught word across the unit, adding them in the lesson's own activities where a text can't carry them, because one meeting teaches little. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#many-encounters-for-each-taught-word)
- Keep several strands of practice running across lessons, with only one bringing in new material while the rest review. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#many-strands-one-of-them-new)
- When the genre changes or texts get harder, model a strategy again with coached practice before independent use, rather than assuming what was learned on stories carries over. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#model-again-when-the-genre-changes)
- Model a reading only after students have read the whole text, and do it beside the text rather than after it. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#model-beside-the-text)
- Link to what comes next by naming the next idea and why it follows, never by position such as 'in the next video', because the order can change. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#name-the-next-idea-not-its-slot)
- Teach the names of a system's parts before explaining how they work together, so learners are not identifying parts while following their relations. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#name-the-parts-before-the-process)
- Nothing in a lesson may preview or point to what the student hasn't reached yet, though a teaser that gives away no events is allowed. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#no-spoilers-of-what-comes-later)
- Close a lesson with three questions on a single idea: name it, find where it works in the text, then use it beyond the text, as support fades. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#one-idea-three-closing-moves)
- Give each unit one explicit primary understanding with its likely misconceptions, and link units by the ideas they share rather than by shared words or names. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#one-understanding-per-unit-linked-by-ideas)
- Open each part of a lesson, and each video, with a line that links it to what the learner has just done. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#open-each-part-from-what-just-happened)
- Build a pre-reading opening around the question the text will answer: pose it early, give the knowledge the reading needs, and close on it to send students into the text. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#open-on-the-question-the-text-answers)
- Order a lesson: what students already know, the source, a worked example, guided tries, their own explanation, a check, and a later transfer. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#order-a-lesson)
- A preview given before new material helps when it stresses the key concepts, not only their structure. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#previews-that-stress-the-key-concepts)
- Show progress as a labelled count a child can read, such as 'Page X of Y', and name a page's internal steps rather than present them as page progress. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#progress-a-child-can-read)
- When one text runs across several lessons, give the last one a task where learners retell or connect the whole, since closing each part alone never asks for it. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#put-a-texts-parts-together-at-the-end)
- Sort reading questions by where the answer lives, in one sentence, across several or with the reader's knowledge, and teach them in that order, each mastered before the next. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#question-kinds-by-where-the-answer-lives)
- Raise one demand at a time: bring in a harder text only once its reading move is known, and withdraw supports singly, so a failed check points to one change. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#raise-one-demand-at-a-time)
- Place quick are-you-reading checks at moments of change, and keep them literal and answerable in about 2 seconds from the sentence just read. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#reading-checks-at-moments-of-change)
- Have a text read more than once, in parts, each pass with its own deeper purpose, rather than read once and quizzed. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#reread-in-passes-that-go-deeper)
- Respond to an unmet prerequisite by its kind: reteach a conceptual gap, give targeted practice for a fluency gap, and give a brief orientation for missing context. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#respond-to-a-gap-by-its-kind)
- Reveal a screen's parts in order, never take away anything that has appeared, and offer the way on only once everything on the page has been seen. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#reveal-in-order-keep-everything-shown)
- Bring early ideas and skills back later, but only with a real upgrade, because a return at the same depth adds practice but no new demand. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#revisit-a-topic-only-with-an-upgrade)
- Early in a teaching script, state what will be learned as one testable sentence and go straight into teaching; a hook or topic label doesn't count. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#say-what-will-be-learned-then-teach)
- Have students pick out the evidence before they put it in their own words or write about it. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#select-the-evidence-first)
- Sequence a course so knowledge and difficulty both progress, since ordering by standard or by readability alone left one of them flat. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#sequence-by-knowledge-and-by-difficulty)
- Revisit each skill at growing intervals on a schedule and mix earlier skills into later practice, so learners must tell which skill an item calls for. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#space-practice-and-mix-in-old-items)
- Bring in a high mastery bar in stages, advisory at first and blocking later, because a flat high bar from the start can leave most learners no way in. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#stage-a-high-mastery-bar)
- Open a course with material students can handle and raise its difficulty in steps. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#start-easy-then-raise-difficulty-in-steps)
- Teach a procedure in full once, and open each later lesson that uses it by recalling it rather than teaching it again from zero. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#teach-a-procedure-once-then-recall-it)
- Teach every new step before a worked example uses it, because a worked example should put together only what is already known. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#teach-a-step-before-an-example-uses-it)
- Teach confusable ideas as a pair: start with clear-cut examples that tell them apart, keep borderline cases for later, contrast them explicitly, and mix them only once both are learned. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#teach-confusable-pairs-explicitly)
- Teach a concept correctly when it first comes up, never skipping it as obvious, and at once state its key constraint and what it is not, with a counterexample. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#teach-it-right-the-first-time)
- Teach the parts of a complex performance in the order they depend on each other, not exam order, so every rubric row is taught before a full task scores it. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#teach-parts-in-dependency-order)
- Teach comprehension strategies briefly and explicitly, inside the content they serve, and spend the rest of the time building knowledge through coherent sets of related texts. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#teach-strategies-briefly-then-build-knowledge)
- Teach the general case before a restricted one, because splitting off the restricted case and teaching it first builds a rule that later has to be undone. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#teach-the-general-case-first)
- When modelling, say what you will do, show it done, then check, putting nothing on screen before it is thought aloud and keeping one target of attention at a time. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#think-aloud-then-show-then-check)
- Make every step the learner hasn't reached unreachable by any input, keyboard included, not just faded from view. [record](groups/making-and-reviewing.md#unreached-steps-take-no-input)
- Weight spaced review toward the skills learners are weakest on, giving them at least half of it, interleaved with the rest. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#weight-review-toward-weak-skills)
- After the first read and before any modelling, have students say what each source contributed, unscored if need be but never accepting a blank. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#what-each-source-gave-before-modelling)
- Don't let young learners skip or speed up instruction on their own judgment, but let them move through each modelled step with a tap rather than on a timer. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#young-learners-dont-choose-what-to-skip)
- Make a procedure's full model compulsory the first time it appears at each stage, then available on request, and shown again automatically after a wrong answer on it. (contested) [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#model-first-then-on-demand)
- Model rejecting a known misconception in the worked example, then retest it cold on a new tempting case, with no hints before the attempt and the repair only in feedback. (contested) [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#model-the-rejection-then-retest-it-cold)
- When learners bring a common wrong belief, draw it out once at the start as a prediction and refute it, since a clear explanation alone can leave it standing. (contested) [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#refute-the-misconception-learners-bring)
- When a learner stumbles in independent work, step back to shared work on the same content rather than moving on, since gradual release runs both ways. (contested) [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#release-runs-both-ways)
- Make the first lesson on a new procedure lighter, with a smaller set to work on, built so later lessons only widen the set instead of rebuilding it. (contested) [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#start-a-new-procedure-light)
- When a later lesson owns a concept's technical name, teach the idea first in plain words and hold the term back, so the name attaches to an idea already held. (contested) [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#teach-the-idea-before-its-name)

## Wording: the language a student reads

- A definition gives the word's general meaning in the word's own grammatical form, without using the word itself or carrying over details from the text where it appears. [record](groups/the-text.md#a-definition-in-the-words-own-form)
- Don't count a glossary pop-up as teaching a word. [record](groups/the-text.md#a-glossary-pop-up-isnt-teaching)
- Write a guide's lines as a welcome, never as a gatekeeper withholding answers, and fix a patronising guide by changing its stance toward the learner, not its words. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#a-guide-welcomes-never-gatekeeps)
- Teach a rule of thumb as a pattern that predicts, not as the reason something happens, because students who take a pattern for a law cannot handle its exceptions. [record](groups/the-text.md#a-pattern-is-not-a-reason)
- Name a strategy's steps in words that work on any text, not one app's mechanics, and present it as the student's own tool for when a text gets hard. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#a-strategy-the-student-owns)
- Teach only words the learner's own text uses more than once and that come back later in the unit. [record](groups/the-text.md#a-taught-word-appears-and-recurs)
- Tie every instruction, hint and status line to the step the learner is on, never an earlier or hidden one, and ask only for what they can do now. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#ask-only-for-what-can-be-done-now)
- Ask what would make anyone act as a character did, not what would make the learner, and check the answer against a clue the text prints. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#ask-what-would-make-anyone-act-so)
- Relay what a speaker in a text claims, insults included, as that speaker's account or prejudice, never as established fact in the lesson's own voice. [record](groups/the-text.md#claims-stay-with-their-speaker)
- Lines that code writes around any text should say only what code can know, such as who speaks or which section comes next, never what the passage means. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#code-written-lines-say-only-what-code-knows)
- Cut vague hedges such as 'maybe' from teaching text but keep the precise quantifiers accuracy needs, such as 'most' or 'some', checking each against the facts. [record](groups/the-text.md#cut-hedges-keep-needed-quantifiers)
- Spend taught-word slots only on words new at the learners' grade, not words graded lists put a grade or more below; give others at most a one-line gloss. [record](groups/the-text.md#deep-word-teaching-only-for-new-targets)
- In science content, say what a thing does and how it happens, never that it exists, acts or changed in order to reach a goal. [record](groups/the-text.md#describe-function-never-purpose)
- Refer to earlier learning in words true whatever a learner was taught before, never as 'last year you learned', and let a course's first lesson assume nothing earlier. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#enrolment-safe-references-to-earlier-learning)
- Pair an example with a non-example that differs only in the defining feature, and say why the non-example fails by naming that feature, never an incidental property. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#example-and-non-example-differ-once)
- Teach a new word with examples from outside the text, easier than the word itself, that don't give away what happens in the text. [record](groups/the-text.md#examples-easier-than-the-word)
- Use examples, units and references the target learners know, or explain them; universal experiences and widely used teaching cases are fine anywhere. [record](groups/the-text.md#examples-the-learners-already-know)
- Define a historical term for a group of people briefly at its first use, and say it plainly and respectfully, with no caricature, stereotype or exoticised accent. [record](groups/the-text.md#historical-terms-defined-briefly-said-plainly)
- Hold back a technical term whose everyday meaning misleads until the concept it names is needed, then define it at once against that everyday meaning. [record](groups/the-text.md#hold-back-a-misleading-term)
- Write instructions a literal reader can follow: verbs match actions, units match what can be selected, and each fill-in frame stays grammatical and sensible for every possible value. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#instructions-a-literal-reader-can-follow)
- Keep fact, interpretation, opinion and uncertainty distinct in what learners read and in the conclusions they frame, so they can tell what kind of claim each statement is. [record](groups/the-text.md#keep-fact-interpretation-and-opinion-apart)
- Write what a lesson's guide says to listen, offer hints, encourage and acknowledge the learner's view, and leave out directives, 'you should' statements and handed-over answers. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#lines-that-support-autonomy)
- Localize a question's surface, such as names, places and everyday objects, but never its concept, its standard units or a context the lesson needs. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#localize-the-surface-not-the-concept)
- Decide on purpose whether a question repeats the text's wording or paraphrases it: a student who only matches words can answer the first but not the second. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#mirror-or-paraphrase-on-purpose)
- Text that models what students should write must be free of errors, because students may copy it. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#modelled-writing-must-be-error-free)
- Don't give learners a branded name for a graphic organiser to remember; name it by its function and say what it asks in plain question language. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#name-an-organiser-by-what-it-asks)
- In narration, name a representation as a representation, give the category before a proper name, and say when something shown is not an example of what is being taught. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#name-each-thing-for-what-it-is)
- When a lesson cites evidence from a text told in parts, name the part and recall the moment by what happens, not by a position like 'the middle'. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#name-the-part-each-clue-comes-from)
- For young students and novices, say the term and show a concrete instance of it together, never one without the other. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#name-the-term-and-show-the-instance)
- Write narration for the ear: introduce a shorthand once and then say it in full, because a repeated bare abbreviation is read aloud as a string of letters. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#narration-written-for-the-ear)
- Never tell learners that the hard part is only now beginning or that what they have done so far does not count. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#never-say-earlier-work-doesnt-count)
- Never show a learner an ability label, tier, group, colour or level name; keep any grouping, levelling and pacing inside the system. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#never-show-a-learner-their-level)
- Never ask a student to say or perform a line with a slur in it: a line a student performs ships only when a judge confirms it is clean. [record](groups/the-text.md#no-slurs-to-perform)
- Write each blank so that only one answer fits, and say whether a term, a phrase or a concept is wanted. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#one-answer-per-blank-and-its-size)
- Never reuse a name for a different kind of thing across lessons read in sequence. [record](groups/the-text.md#one-name-one-kind-of-thing)
- Every line a student reads gets one plain voice: no praise, no exclamation marks, no verdict the screen already shows, and at most one question, none in a closing line. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#one-plain-voice-for-every-line)
- Pitch a model answer at what a strong student of the grade could write in the time, not at expert writing. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#pitch-model-answers-at-a-strong-student)
- Name each step and concept one way everywhere, in plain words for what a reader thinks or does, with no internal jargon or stock assistant phrasing. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#plain-consistent-words-for-each-step)
- Never point anyone reading or marking an item at what they cannot see, like an off-screen label, code, highlight or line, or the brief and source facts behind it. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#point-only-at-what-is-on-screen)
- Before work begins, show the learner its purpose, the first action and what success will look like, in words the learner can read, not only in review documents. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#purpose-first-action-and-success-up-front)
- Give each taught word or concept one complete definition of its real meaning, not an incidental property, used word for word wherever it returns, keeping words taught together distinct. [record](groups/the-text.md#same-words-every-time)
- Say each thing once and only to teach: no line restating the printed question or anything already said or shown, explaining the obvious, or competing with the learner's action. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#say-each-thing-once)
- A lesson says plainly what its source cannot show, and models declining an inference the source does not support. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#say-what-a-source-cant-show)
- Introduce new words through the lesson's purpose and the whole text they serve, not in isolation, and say when a word carries a theme beyond one character or lesson. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#say-why-a-new-word-matters)
- Write instructions, prompts, feedback and anything a lesson's guide says at an easier reading level than the text they support, and keep internal design terms out of them. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#scaffolds-easier-to-read-than-the-text)
- Cap every sentence a student must act on, from instructions and questions to hints and feedback, at about 25 words. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#sentences-of-25-words-at-most)
- Use emphasis sparingly and consistently, with no all-caps, because emphasis on one fragment can tell learners that only that fragment needs an answer. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#sparing-consistent-emphasis)
- Strip drafting, self-checking and debug text from an item before a student or a judge sees it. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#strip-the-working-before-it-ships)
- Use no word students may not know before the lesson teaches it, narration counting as teaching, and teach technical words directly, since context fails when the subject is new. [record](groups/the-text.md#teach-a-word-before-using-it)
- A true explanation can still teach wrongly: check it for circular definitions, misleading analogies, contradictions, unconnected facts and wording that invites a misconception, allowing simplification normal for the level. [record](groups/the-text.md#true-explanations-that-still-mislead)
- Teach and gloss the words that carry what a reader needs to know, not the rarest ones, each for its sense in its own line. [record](groups/the-text.md#words-that-carry-the-knowledge)
- Point at a text by its words, never its line numbers: editions number lines differently, and a lesson may show none. [record](groups/the-text.md#words-not-line-numbers)
- Introduce a new word by giving its meaning first and its label last, never by asking learners to guess what it means. (contested) [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#meaning-first-the-word-last)
- Name each procedure step in its own words, on screen and aloud, never by a number, which only signals unseen steps, and say the procedure's name whenever it runs. (contested) [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#name-the-steps-never-number-them)
- Pre-teach only words without which comprehension breaks down; support the rest in place, replace them or teach them after reading, and never introduce a word by asking what it means. (contested) [record](groups/the-text.md#pre-teach-only-the-words-that-block-reading)

## Integrity: leaks, cues, gaming and truthfulness

- A rule a lesson presents as true in general must hold for every case the lesson itself teaches. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#a-general-rule-holds-for-every-case)
- Judge whether a visible support such as a picture or diagram gives the answer away by the item's role and audience. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#a-giveaway-depends-on-role-and-audience)
- A question meant to test subject knowledge fails if a reader without that knowledge can reach the answer from what the item gives. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#a-question-must-need-the-knowledge)
- A question about a text must need the text. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#a-question-must-need-the-text)
- A button tells the truth: it names what it does, never claims understanding or previews the next page, and when locked stays visible and says in words what unlocks it. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#buttons-tell-the-truth)
- State in each item's specification the cueing it intends (none, support, diagnostic or authentic), and treat any cue it doesn't declare as a defect. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#declare-each-items-cueing-intent)
- No step may use up what a later one teaches or tests. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#dont-spend-a-later-steps-evidence-early)
- Everything a lesson says, quoted or not, from model answers and feedback to marking guides, must be true to its source and claim no more than the source's words support. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#every-line-true-to-the-source)
- In a two-part evidence item, hinge the first part on a detail only the passage gives, and keep the keyed evidence from announcing itself or containing that part's answer. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#evidence-that-doesnt-give-itself-away)
- Keep what is meant for adults, such as timers, build strings, counters and skip or review switches, off the learner's screen. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#keep-adult-controls-off-the-learners-screen)
- Keep a scored test's item bank behind sign-in, because an open route lets anyone harvest its items. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#keep-the-item-bank-behind-sign-in)
- Keep keys, rationales, rubrics and correction routes off the learner's device, even hidden, until each response is submitted, and score on the server, never trusting the screen's correctness flag. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#keys-stay-on-the-server)
- Make the thinking a task targets, such as understanding a text rather than matching its words, impossible to skip: students take any shortcut, and a gamed pass proves nothing. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#make-the-target-thinking-unavoidable)
- Award mastery, progress and rewards only for verified learning: passive activity, clicking through, revealed answers, guessing, replays and low-accuracy work earn nothing, and detected gaming costs points. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#mastery-only-for-verified-learning)
- Don't present AI classmates as real peers, since learners got better at unmasking them through social and contextual inconsistency rather than language. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#never-pass-ai-classmates-off-as-peers)
- Nothing a student sees or hears may give the answer before the first attempt, including the picture behind a paused question. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#nothing-gives-the-answer-first)
- Every number in a question's setup must be possible and consistent with the others, and the reasoning to the answer must rest on a true premise, not just correct arithmetic. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#possible-numbers-and-a-sound-premise)
- A lesson's promises about what comes next must hold in the lessons that follow; reword one they don't keep as something the learner will do. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#promise-only-what-later-lessons-keep)
- Controls for previewing or reviewing a lesson, such as free navigation, answer reveal and reset, leave mastery, rewards, completion and unlocks exactly as they were. [record](groups/making-and-reviewing.md#reviewer-controls-leave-no-evidence)
- Tell and record only what actually happened: no canned line posing as a response, no step the student didn't take, and an end screen that claims completion, never mastery. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#say-only-what-happened)
- Simplify without saying or implying anything false, keeping the same conceptual foundation at every level and presenting every model or diagram as a simplification, never as literal reality. [record](groups/the-text.md#simplify-without-saying-anything-false)
- Content must stay consistent with every detail it states about a person or scenario. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#stay-true-to-the-stated-scenario)
- Decide whether an item checks taught knowledge, where overlap with the teaching is the point, or independent use of a skill, which needs evidence the teaching never used. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#transfer-work-needs-evidence-the-teaching-didnt-use)
- Even where answers are kept and never marked, refuse noise such as gibberish, the question given back or a copied line, and ask plainly for the student's own answer. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#unmarked-answers-still-refuse-noise)
- Write new questions rather than copying or paraphrasing released exam items. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#write-new-items-not-released-ones)
- When lesson machinery fails, let the learner carry on rather than trap them, but credit only what they did, by a typed answer or fallback check, never on trust. (contested) [record](groups/checking-answers.md#fail-open-for-progress-closed-for-credit)
- Hold the question until a learner reading alone could plausibly have read the text, timed from its word count at a generous reading rate for their age. (contested) [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#hold-the-check-until-the-text-is-read)
- Keep what the text says apart from what the student infers: an inference never fills the slot for the text's words and never sits inside quotation marks. (contested) [record](groups/the-text.md#keep-the-text-apart-from-the-inference)

## Accessibility: access for every learner

- Let no access need block participation, use equivalent access modes only where what is measured stays unchanged, so reading-test passages are never read aloud, and disclose supports that change it. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#access-without-changing-what-is-measured)
- Every interaction a student uses must work by keyboard and assistive technology and be legible, checked from the start, because an item a learner can't operate yields an invalid score. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#accessible-interaction-types-from-the-start)
- Announce each new piece of feedback once to assistive technology and keep restored earlier feedback quiet, so a screen-reader user never hears an old verdict as new. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#announce-each-new-verdict-once)
- Keep captions readable and check it in code: a capped reading rate and line length, at most two lines, and never covering the visual being explained. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#captions-a-viewer-can-read-in-time)
- No control or pop-up may cover reading text or leave the screen at any width, even after a resize. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#controls-never-cover-text-or-leave-the-screen)
- Set every line that tells a learner what to do, hints and help included, at a contrast of at least 4.5:1, in sentence case and upright type. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#every-instruction-legible-at-451)
- Fit the text, the task and the controls on one screen without page scroll at the most constrained size learners really use, measured on a real device. [record](groups/making-and-reviewing.md#fit-the-most-constrained-real-screen)
- Keep a word being taught in the same place through every stage of its routine, reserving room from the start for any control that appears later. [record](groups/making-and-reviewing.md#hold-a-taught-word-in-place)
- Keep an on-screen reading passage's lines to about 55–75 characters, even on a wide screen. [record](groups/the-text.md#moderate-line-length-for-screen-passages)
- No state, such as a reload mid-step, a failed 3D or media part, or help that has run out, may leave a learner without a way on. [record](groups/making-and-reviewing.md#no-dead-ends-for-the-learner)
- Keep an item solvable without local or cultural knowledge the course doesn't teach, in a neutral everyday setting unless the curriculum calls for a particular one. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#no-local-knowledge-the-course-doesnt-teach)
- Make every page work with narration off: whatever the narration says or reveals must also be reachable by the learner's own tap or a transcript, never only by listening. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#nothing-reachable-only-by-listening)
- Give every animated picture a reduced-motion version with the same information and a text description, and never let colour carry meaning alone. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#reduced-motion-and-colour)
- When progress is locked, show the learner which task is holding it, along with their position in the lesson and the reading mode they are in. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#show-which-task-holds-the-learner)
- When a worked example marks a rejected option, style the option itself and keep the explanation of why it fails fully readable. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#strike-the-option-not-its-explanation)
- Let students hear every hard word in a passage and see what it means, not only the taught words, so no question turns on a word they were never given. [record](groups/the-text.md#support-every-hard-word-in-a-passage)
- Give every diagram a task uses a text equivalent carrying the same information as the picture and no more, since a rule or role word there gives the answer away. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#text-equivalents-match-the-picture-no-more)
- Give a spoken-answer task a text fallback of equal status, and keep speech-recognition errors, accent and noise from counting against the content. (contested) [record](groups/writing-questions.md#a-text-fallback-for-spoken-answers)

## Media: pictures, sound and film

- A film's captions, transcript and checks must match the film that plays, so they change with it. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#a-film-changes-with-everything-tied-to-it)
- An on-screen guide adds little by its presence or look, least for older learners, so spend the effort on what it says, in a conversational register. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#a-guides-words-not-its-looks)
- A picture, map or chart in an item, or the description it is made from, must name the specific labels, routes, axes, values or captions its questions use. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#a-visual-names-what-its-questions-use)
- When the skill is reading a diagram, make its elements selectable and include steps answered on the diagram itself, such as tracing a path, not only sentences picked about it. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#answer-on-the-diagram-not-only-about-it)
- Build a complex diagram one element at a time as each is named, rather than opening on the finished, fully labelled picture. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#build-a-complex-diagram-up-step-by-step)
- Give a teaching film switchable captions on a separate track, never burned into the picture, keep a transcript, and keep on-screen text to the labels that teach. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#captions-on-a-track-not-in-the-picture)
- Break any long film that carries content a student needs with required quick checks, placed only at natural boundaries, and put a short film's check straight after it. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#checks-at-boundaries-after-short-films)
- Set a quotation card in a film without a one-word last line, and start a marked word's underline on the word, not on the space before it. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#clean-setting-for-quotation-cards)
- Choose a mark's colour for what the mark means, never by its order in a list. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#colour-each-mark-for-its-meaning)
- When a picture contrasts two states, keep the framing and scale identical in both halves so that only the contrast changes. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#contrasts-keep-framing-and-scale)
- Cue the one element the narration is explaining at that moment, with a brief accent or arrow, so learners never have to search for it. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#cue-the-part-being-explained)
- Cut anything in a teaching video that cannot name the fact it teaches or protects. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#every-second-teaches-or-protects-a-fact)
- Give every visual difference on screen a meaning and a key, or drop it, and never let one look, such as a highlight colour, stand for two things. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#every-visual-difference-carries-a-stated-meaning)
- Give every word card the same shape: when pictures fit only a few taught words, use none, since uneven cards signal that some words are harder or different. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#every-word-card-the-same-shape)
- Put few words on screen, the ones the voice is saying as it says them: in one study, two or three per slide helped retention. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#few-words-on-screen)
- A film about a text says only what the text supports, with no labels for its characters that the text doesn't bear out. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#films-true-to-the-text)
- Keep a studied text's slurs and hateful lines on the page only, never voiced, staged or lit, and skip whole sentences so no fragment of one is performed. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#hateful-lines-stay-on-the-page)
- A generated narration voice never imitates a real person's voice or an existing character's voice. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#imitate-no-real-or-existing-voice)
- Judge a narration voice by its register and pace rather than by whether it is synthetic, since modern synthetic voices taught adults as well as recorded human ones. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#judge-a-synthetic-voice-by-register)
- Keep instructional video segments to about six minutes or less. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#keep-video-segments-short)
- When a picture or film introduces several people, make each visually distinct and label each with name and role on first appearance, as overlaid text, not narration alone. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#label-each-character-when-it-first-appears)
- Put each label beside the part it names, with a short leader line, at the moment the narration names it, never in a legend or caption bar. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#label-each-part-as-it-is-named)
- Label every image truthfully: a real photograph or painting with its credit and what it really shows, never as a story's own place, and an invented illustration as an illustration. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#label-every-image-for-what-it-is)
- Leave silence after a narrated question meant to make the listener think, before the next line, music or activity begins. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#leave-silence-after-a-thinking-question)
- Give a synthetic human on screen a face, voice and motion that match, since a mismatch between them, more than realism, is what makes it read as fake. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#match-face-voice-and-motion)
- Give every media element a working fallback, so a clip, image or video that fails leaves readable text or the screens it duplicates instead of blocking the learner. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#media-failure-never-blocks-learning)
- Media must not run ahead of the text: an opening film never reveals the ending, and a picture never shows a character or event before the text reaches it. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#media-never-runs-ahead-of-the-text)
- When a narration clip is missing, fail closed: show its text in silence rather than fall back to a synthetic voice. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#missing-media-fails-closed)
- Narrate in a conversational second-person voice that sounds human, not robotic and not in a formal textbook register. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#narrate-in-a-human-sounding-conversational-voice)
- Make each narration line add what the picture cannot show, rather than describe what the frame already makes obvious. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#narration-adds-what-the-picture-cant)
- Let narration and captions give the facts students need, such as who people are and where events happen, and leave what the pictures imply for students to infer. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#narration-orients-pictures-imply)
- Play a question's audio only when the student asks for it, never before they have read the question. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#narration-that-waits-for-the-reader)
- Never gate progress on a background video that repeats on-screen teaching, since a required video walls off the learners who most need the lesson. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#never-gate-progress-on-a-background-video)
- Never loop or replay a generated clip to fill a teaching film's narration, since each loop visibly resets mid-sentence. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#never-loop-a-clip-to-fill-narration)
- Put no answer text on a picture or diagram. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#no-answer-on-the-diagram)
- Attach a picture only when the task uses it, and supply every picture, chart, document or clip the task points to, since a description in words doesn't take its place. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#no-decorative-or-missing-pictures)
- Never let imagery on a teaching screen pose as an instance of the concept being taught, such as a character's picture beside a word it doesn't exemplify. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#no-picture-as-a-false-instance)
- Never ship anything that looks like a placeholder: use the real asset, or redesign the page without it and note what is missing. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#nothing-that-looks-like-a-placeholder)
- In a film that explains, show one main visual action at a time, with no eye-catching motion behind the explanation. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#one-visual-action-at-a-time)
- Never let two audio sources play at once, such as music or sound effects under teaching narration, and keep the microphone closed while narration plays. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#one-voice-at-a-time-mic-closed)
- Read the text in one voice and the teaching around it in another, keeping each role's voice the same in every lesson so students hear them as different things. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#one-voice-reads-another-teaches)
- Leave off a young child's media player any control they can't interpret, such as skip-five-seconds buttons, and give one large play and pause control instead. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#only-controls-a-young-child-understands)
- Never let a caption, control, card, panel border or crop cover or cut what the learner still needs, such as teaching text, a clue or a character's face. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#overlays-stay-off-what-learners-need)
- A person in a generated picture or film must not look machine-made, so that no student dismisses the material as fake and skips it. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#people-in-generated-pictures-must-not-look-fake)
- A picture beside a quotation must not imply the line is spoken, or a character present, where the text doesn't place them, and notes record where each quotation comes from. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#pictures-must-not-misplace-a-quoted-line)
- Keep pictures out of every reading and question gate, so adding art never changes a page's pacing or reveals a question early, and recheck the gates after any art change. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#pictures-never-change-a-gate)
- Prefer a picture that carries information, such as a map or a comparison chart, to one that only decorates. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#pictures-that-carry-information)
- A picture should show what the voice is saying. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#pictures-that-show-what-is-said)
- A teaching video should have the viewer predict or tell things apart before it gives an answer, and end on a prompted recall rather than a summary. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#predict-first-recall-last)
- For fluent or upper-grade readers, as in Grade 9 literature, keep read-aloud of the text off by default but available on request, so reading time goes to unaided reading. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#read-aloud-off-by-default-for-proficient-readers)
- Use a real image for any real place, object or document students may have to recognise, not a generated likeness. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#real-images-of-real-things)
- When text or a model answer appears bit by bit, reveal it in whole words or chunks in step with the explanation, not by slow letter-by-letter tracing. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#reveal-words-not-strokes)
- Design each student screen for its learners' age without infantilizing them: no childish visuals or wording, and no clutter, pop-ups or decoration that obscures learning. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#right-for-the-age-never-childish)
- A picture or caption that puts separate episodes in one scene can teach that they are connected, so show separate times or places as separate, labelled scenes. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#separate-episodes-separate-scenes)
- When the skill is working with a medium itself, such as reading a map or listening, the item must present that medium; for reasoning about content, text will do. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#show-the-medium-the-skill-uses)
- When a word is sounded out in parts, voice each part as it sounds inside the word, in the same voice as the whole word. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#sound-out-parts-as-parts-of-the-word)
- When a routine has the learner reread a stretch of text, narrow the view to it and pause narration until they say they have, so they do the reading. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#stop-the-voice-for-a-reread)
- An imaginative illustration is never evidence a question relies on: keep it to what the text has shown, and never draw inferred thoughts as facts. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#story-art-is-never-evidence)
- Show a guide's face or name only beside words the learner can actually hear, so a reader reaching for the audio is never offered a voice that doesn't exist. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#the-guides-face-promises-a-voice)
- When a task pairs a text with an image, performance or adaptation, establish the text's meaning first, so the other medium can't replace reading it. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#the-texts-meaning-before-its-adaptation)
- Give a gesturing on-screen guide a human voice, since in one experiment the benefit of human-like gesture held with a human voice but not with a machine voice. (contested) [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#a-gesturing-guide-needs-a-human-voice)
- Keep a live view of the learner's own face out of a lesson, because seeing oneself can raise appearance anxiety and lower recall. (contested) [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#no-live-self-view-while-learning)
- Read text aloud at one fixed pace set for the grade, not per learner, and let learners pause and replay rather than slowing the voice, which distorts speech. (contested) [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#one-fixed-read-aloud-pace-with-replay)

## Practice: how learners practise and retry

- A modelled answer must answer the question asked, picking one of the choices an either-or question offers, never defining the question's words or answering a neighbouring question. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#a-modelled-answer-answers-its-question)
- Keep a commit-before-reveal prompt, such as saying a word's meaning before turning its card, as retrieval practice, but never count it as assessment, since nothing checks the answer. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#a-recall-prompt-that-isnt-assessment)
- In independent practice, still walk the taught procedure, giving each step a small task that shows the learner did it. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#a-task-that-proves-each-step)
- Design and test an item's allowed attempts, feedback steps, marking criteria, reveal and service-failure exit together, so no attempt runs past the written feedback or strands the learner. [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#attempts-feedback-and-reveal-as-one-design)
- No optimisation, whether spacing, interleaving, multimedia or game elements, makes up for missing basics: unambiguous instruction, retrieval practice and mastery gating. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#basics-before-optimisations)
- Practise a new skill on its own while it is being learned, and mix it into review only after it passes a retest on new items on a later day. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#block-first-mix-after-a-later-retest)
- Build a few bounded, meaningful choices into every lesson, such as which text or question comes first and how to respond, so learners bring their interests into it. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#bounded-choices-in-every-lesson)
- Build practice up to the length and load of the performance it prepares for, because mastering short pieces one at a time does not build sustained work. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#build-up-to-the-targets-length)
- Have learners check a meaning they worked out from context by swapping it into the sentence: if the sentence no longer makes sense, the meaning is wrong. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#check-a-meaning-by-swapping-it-in)
- Give guided practice that compares and corrects: students pick the better of two responses and say why, or stop a model answer at a planted error and fix it. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#compare-answers-catch-a-planted-error)
- Have learners cut a scene to half its length and defend the most important line they kept and the most useless one they cut: deciding what stays forces close reading. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#cut-a-scene-to-read-it-closely)
- Run the worked example, the guided practice and the independent task on different material, so no stage answers another and the last one tests transfer, not rehearsal. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#each-stage-on-fresh-material)
- Spend most of a course teaching and building knowledge rather than drilling the final test, keeping test-format practice to set points late in the sequence. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#early-practice-doesnt-look-like-the-test)
- Don't rely on unguided independent reading time to raise reading outcomes, since a meta-analysis found that simply encouraging more independent reading did not improve them. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#free-reading-time-is-not-instruction)
- Gate every check: one a student can skip is decoration, so a seek past an unanswered question goes back to it. [record](groups/pictures-sound-film.md#gate-every-check)
- In guided practice the lesson supplies part of the answer, never the conclusion, and the learner finishes the reasoning; after one miss rephrase, after two offer a choice. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#guided-practice-is-shared-work)
- Keep practice apart from assessment: checks during teaching give feedback and support without a mastery bar, and only a later scored assessment, with supports off, holds students to it. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#keep-practice-apart-from-assessment)
- Let learners teach an AI peer, explaining or defending a position to it, since expecting to explain to someone else produces deeper processing than studying to be tested. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#let-learners-teach-an-ai-peer)
- In an argument task, let the worked example argue the side the learner did not pick, so it models the reasoning without handing over their case. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#model-the-side-the-learner-didnt-pick)
- Teach comprehension monitoring as two steps, noticing a breakdown by trying to say what the part is about, then rereading to repair it, not as an abstract instruction to monitor. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#notice-a-breakdown-then-reread-to-repair)
- Modelling, guided practice and independent practice should run the same skill under the same step names, with support that only falls, so they read as one process with labels stripped. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#one-process-falling-support)
- Practise a stable fact with brief, frequent retrieval that tapers off, and understanding that depends on a schema by retrieval, feedback, then use in a new task. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#practise-facts-and-schemas-differently)
- After study, practise taught words by typed recall: cue each by its meaning in a one-blank sentence pointing to it alone and containing no form of it; nudge before revealing. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#practise-taught-words-by-typed-recall)
- Let learners practise the formats and interactions of the test that counts before it counts, because meeting an unfamiliar interface on the scored test adds noise to the score. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#practise-the-formats-before-they-count)
- In a retrieval warm-up, have learners answer aloud before any options appear, and show the options only afterwards as a check, so they recall rather than recognise. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#recall-aloud-before-the-options-appear)
- Reteach when a learner's gain is flat, not merely when accuracy is low. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#reteach-on-flat-gain-not-low-accuracy)
- After a miss, reteach what failed, retest on new parallel items or a fresh test form, never the same ones, check again later, and send repeated failure to placement review. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#reteach-then-retest-on-new-items)
- Make retrieval ask for something taught earlier after a gap, with it hidden, because a check straight after teaching shows only that the student was following. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#retrieval-needs-a-gap)
- Use low-stakes retrieval questions as learning events, not only as measures, because practising recall beats restudying. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#retrieval-questions-teach-not-just-measure)
- Route learners who need more comprehension help by their specific need, vocabulary and background knowledge, strategy or text structure, not by a global reading level. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#route-by-the-need-not-the-level)
- Teach summarising as a fixed routine for each chunk: mark the key who or what, keep what matters, write one new short sentence, and check it against the marks. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#summarise-each-chunk-by-fixed-steps)
- Instruction must contain at least one concrete claim from which a check question with a definite answer and plausible wrong options can be written; otherwise no retrieval can follow it. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#teach-a-claim-a-question-can-check)
- Teach writing through explicit, form-focused revision, such as worked revisions of a weak answer into a full-marks one against named criteria, rather than by prompting students to write. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#teach-revision-not-just-prompts-to-write)
- Teach learners to unpack a hard sentence by fixed questions: who or what, doing what, to whom, and why that word. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#unpack-a-hard-sentence-by-fixed-questions)
- Have students write about what they read, since writing about a text raises reading comprehension, most for lower-achieving readers. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#write-about-what-was-read)
- Model each step in the interaction learners will later use for it, because a step only narrated in the model leaves them performing a move they have never seen. (contested) [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#model-a-step-in-its-own-interaction)
- Give every screen a response that does the teaching work, and let no passive stretch of watching, hearing or reading run past about a minute without one. (contested) [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#something-to-do-on-every-screen)
- Two tries, then the answer; otherwise a student who never gets it right can't finish. (contested) [record](groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#two-tries-then-the-answer)

## Evidence of learning: what shows that learning happened

- End each block with an unseen text running a known procedure on new content, with no model or sentence starters, and let it decide stepping up or re-modelling. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#a-cold-text-decides-each-step-up)
- An item used to diagnose must test one piece of knowledge, because a wrong answer to a bundled item can't say what is missing. [record](groups/writing-questions.md#a-diagnostic-item-measures-one-thing)
- Use a diagnostic to route support under stated uncertainty, never as a grade or a mastery label. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#a-diagnostic-routes-support-never-grades)
- Move a learner on only when rigorous checks of independent, unprompted performance show mastery, never for time spent, finishing or saying they know it. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#advance-on-mastery-not-completion)
- Assess what reading produced in knowledge gained, its application and engagement, not only recall, gist or word-meaning tasks. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#assess-knowledge-application-and-engagement)
- Decide whether a learner has understood from what they do on a task, not from their own sense of it, because readers judge their own comprehension poorly. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#check-understanding-with-a-task)
- Count a creative or performance product as evidence of reading only when its reasoning is inspectable, for instance through an attached justification of its choices from the text. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#creative-work-counts-when-its-reasoning-shows)
- Credit only what a student showed in the mode being assessed: a written plan is not evidence of a spoken performance. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#credit-only-what-was-shown)
- Base a mastery decision on several different items from several item families, because passing by retrying near-identical items, or on a handful of items, is practice, not mastery. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#mastery-needs-breadth-across-item-families)
- Give each lesson one key idea and close on it, with the student restating it, finding evidence for it in the text and applying it as support fades. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#one-key-idea-restated-at-the-close)
- Anything taught needs graded work where learners produce it, as picking, recognising, mentioning or echoing it never counts, and no earlier check may use a taught word as its answer. [record](groups/how-people-learn.md#produce-it-dont-just-pick-it)
- Restore a learner's place after a reload, Back or mode switch only from a record of what they actually did, so returning neither erases earned progress nor grants unearned credit. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#restore-only-what-the-learner-did)

## Marking: judging a student's own words

- Count a retell or summary only if it is shorter than the source and uses at least one word the source doesn't; a copied sentence fails, however apt. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#a-retell-is-shorter-in-new-words)
- Decide first whether an answer is a real attempt: never grade nonsense, a joke or a refusal, and if no attempt comes, end with a model answer. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#decide-first-whether-it-is-an-attempt)
- If automated marking fails, spend no try and give no verdict; after repeated failures, show the authored answer, let the learner move on, and label it unchecked, not earned. [record](groups/ai-judges-and-markers.md#failed-marking-never-traps-a-learner)
- Hold each stage to its own demands: never ask beginners for a later stage's features or finer terms, count extras as strengths, and never withhold credit for a misnamed term. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#hold-each-stage-to-its-own-demands)
- In a word web, accept any word a reader could reasonably link to the centre word, leave spelling unmarked, and don't count random words or names. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#mark-a-word-web-generously)
- Put no length floor or ceiling on a learner's answer: a short right answer is secure, and independent practice, writing included, is never capped in time or space. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#no-length-floor-or-ceiling)

## Measurement: how quality and learning are measured

- A mastery gate needs enough items to mean anything, so check what its bar demands at each test's real length: on four or five items, 90% means a perfect score. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#a-gate-needs-enough-items)
- A test that must measure learners across several grades should adapt to each one, since a fixed form of normal length spread over that range is imprecise everywhere. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#a-test-across-grades-must-adapt)
- In an adaptive test, require an answer before moving on and lock it once given, since later items are chosen from it and going back breaks the selection. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#answer-before-advancing-then-lock-it)
- Report every score, sub-score or mastery estimate with its own uncertainty, such as a standard error or a floor or ceiling flag, never as a bare number. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#report-a-score-with-its-uncertainty)
- Spread a test's items across easy, medium and hard, since an all-medium form puts weak students at the floor and strong ones at the ceiling. [record](groups/checking-answers.md#spread-difficulty-across-easy-medium-and-hard)

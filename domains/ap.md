# AP

10 learnings that hold in AP work rather than everywhere. The universal ones are in [PERFECT-QUESTION.md](../PERFECT-QUESTION.md) and [GENERATING.md](../GENERATING.md).

## What a perfect question is, here

### Stimulus: the text, document or source a question rests on

- When a source is there to call up knowledge rather than to test reading, each question should need both the source and that knowledge. [record](../groups/writing-questions.md#a-source-that-calls-for-knowledge)
- Explain a historical development as conditions, motives, capabilities and choices working together, keeping what made it desirable apart from what made it possible, never one cause alone. [record](../groups/the-text.md#motives-capabilities-and-choices-together)
- When a question rests on a primary source, show students who wrote it and why, and cite it accurately. [record](../groups/the-text.md#show-who-wrote-a-source-and-why)

### Demand: the thinking a task requires

- Treat dates as limits on cause: mark each period, count a date usable only when its position matters to an argument, and use later events only for change or significance. [record](../groups/how-people-learn.md#dates-limit-what-can-cause-what)

### Sequence: order and spacing across a lesson or course

- Order a history unit as context, the minimum model, complications that defeat an over-simple account, source work, argument, and a synthesis that answers the unit's question. [record](../groups/how-people-learn.md#order-a-history-unit-toward-its-synthesis)
- Teach source analysis in five steps: what it shows, the bounded claim this supports, the outside knowledge needed, how its maker and purpose matter, and what needs corroboration. [record](../groups/how-people-learn.md#source-work-from-evidence-to-use)

### Wording: the language a student reads

- Bound every historical claim by actor, region, period, institution and source function, and say for whom and how far it held, rather than flattening history into a generic narrative. [record](../groups/the-text.md#bound-each-historical-claim)

## Context

- From May 2027 the Advanced Placement United States History exam has source-based multiple-choice sets, sourced short answers, a document-based essay question and a broad long essay. [record](../groups/writing-questions.md#the-ap-us-history-exams-shape)
- Can a machine marker score AP-style short answers and essays reliably enough to award mastery, once calibrated against independently scored responses? [record](../groups/not-settled.md#machine-marks-on-ap-free-responses)
- Whether questions with no source to read serve practice as well as source-based ones is untested: a judge passed both, but only the source-based form matches the exam. [record](../groups/not-settled.md#practice-items-without-a-source)

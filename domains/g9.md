# Grade 9

27 learnings that hold in Grade 9 work rather than everywhere. The universal ones are in [PERFECT-QUESTION.md](../PERFECT-QUESTION.md) and [GENERATING.md](../GENERATING.md).

## What a perfect question is, here

### Stem: the question itself

- End a literature lesson's closing questions on a choice between two defensible readings that learners must justify, since a question naming the reading it wants asks only for description. [record](../groups/writing-questions.md#close-on-a-choice-between-two-readings)

### Stimulus: the text, document or source a question rests on

- In a literature unit, fix a set of must-teach quotations, mark them in the reading text, and build each lesson's guided practice on them. [record](../groups/the-text.md#a-fixed-set-of-must-teach-quotations)
- Show each extract from a play with its speaker and its line reference, in the numbering of the edition learners see, converting any other numbering first. [record](../groups/the-text.md#cite-each-play-extract-by-speaker-and-line)

### Scoring guide: the rubric or marking guide for written answers

- Mark an analytical paragraph on four jobs, each checked alone: a claim answering the question, a quotation, what a quoted word suggests, and a link back to the question. [record](../groups/checking-answers.md#four-jobs-of-an-analytical-paragraph)

### Feedback: what a student is told after answering

- While learners write on their own, mark their writing live in small, frequent touches, such as a word to swap, so they improve the text as they go. [record](../groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#mark-independent-writing-live)

### Format: the kind of task

- Ask how a line should be spoken only where it allows clearly different deliveries and the lines around it show what the speaker wants, which decides the key. [record](../groups/writing-questions.md#delivery-questions-need-choice-and-context)
- Let students try contrasting readings or change one element of a scene, predict what changes for characters and audience, and explain why, before any finished interpretation is given. [record](../groups/how-people-learn.md#experiment-with-the-text-before-interpreting-it)

### Demand: the thinking a task requires

- Ask why a writer structured a text as they did, not how many paragraphs or headings it has, since the state reading test rewards explaining the writer's choices. [record](../groups/writing-questions.md#ask-about-the-writers-structural-choices)

### Sequence: order and spacing across a lesson or course

- Feed students' answers to a film's in-video checks into the next lesson's retrieval, designing film and lesson together, without repeating what the current lesson's retrieval asks. [record](../groups/how-people-learn.md#film-checks-feed-later-retrieval)
- Keep every component in every lesson and vary only its time, by lesson type and learner, with floors: support shrinks but never vanishes, and retrieval keeps about a tenth. [record](../groups/how-people-learn.md#fixed-components-flexible-time)
- In a course's first lesson, call up prior knowledge through the unit's key concept, such as a word web hinting at last year's text, not straight retrieval from it. [record](../groups/how-people-learn.md#open-a-unit-through-its-key-concept)
- Narrate a long text read in parts: open each part with a short line on what to watch for, review it afterwards, and summarise at the end. [record](../groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#narrate-between-the-sections-of-a-long-text)

### Wording: the language a student reads

- For adolescents, pitch a reading skill as a way to resist being manipulated, catching how a text works on the reader, rather than as practice of a skill. [record](../groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#frame-skills-as-resisting-manipulation)
- At the lowest stage, name words only by a few simple classes and devices, such as noun, verb or metaphor, and keep finer terms for the top stage. [record](../groups/what-a-student-is-told.md#simple-word-classes-at-the-lowest-stage)
- Teach dramatic irony through a line the audience hears and the deceived character does not, keeping what the audience knows apart from what each character knows. [record](../groups/how-people-learn.md#teach-dramatic-irony-through-a-line)

### Media: pictures, sound and film

- Set a ceiling on the share of a reading lesson given to knowledge-building film and audio, and measure that share on the built lesson. [record](../groups/pictures-sound-film.md#a-ceiling-on-knowledge-building-media)
- Don't let a held picture stand in for a performed passage, because a still in its place changes what the passage is. [record](../groups/pictures-sound-film.md#a-still-cant-replace-a-performance)
- Give a still scene meant for inference rich detail and meaningful activity beyond the foreground, leave its implications unexplained, and have students support an interpretation with what they noticed. [record](../groups/pictures-sound-film.md#scenes-that-leave-something-to-notice)
- To teach how performance shapes meaning, have learners compare two performances of one scene, judge what each delivery does to a character and support it with the words. [record](../groups/pictures-sound-film.md#two-performances-that-differ-only-in-delivery)
- Name a text's violent events plainly and seriously, without sensation, but never show the act or picture death or graphic harm. [record](../groups/pictures-sound-film.md#violence-named-plainly-never-shown)
- Use a synthetic voice only for connective narration and give readings of the text to human voices, since learners can't catch its mispronunciations in verse or older English. [record](../groups/pictures-sound-film.md#synthetic-voice-for-connective-narration-only)
- An opening film may state a story's ending plainly on purpose, without showing it, so the unit's question becomes how the ending comes about, for students to trace. [record](../groups/pictures-sound-film.md#tell-the-ending-on-purpose)

## How to generate them, here

### 2. Write: draft the stimulus, question, options, guide and feedback

- Build a short film on a text as a voiced argument, one visible claim at a time: the key line quoted exactly, what it shows, then two defensible readings. [record](../groups/pictures-sound-film.md#a-film-argues-one-claim-at-a-time)
- Abridge by cutting sections, keep the author's words for what stays, bridge each cut with a bracketed summary, and say the text is abridged and from which edition. [record](../groups/the-text.md#bracket-a-summary-where-lines-are-cut)
- When describing a person for a generated picture, name hair, glasses and clothes, never skin tone. [record](../groups/pictures-sound-film.md#describe-people-without-skin-tone)

## Context

- Wrong options a model writes rarely pass a judge's test of whether they would tempt a skimming reader. [record](../groups/generating-with-a-model.md#model-written-wrong-options-rarely-tempt)
- Whether learners should meet a hard archaic text first in a modern version, beside one, or only in the original with glosses of key words is unsettled. [record](../groups/not-settled.md#modern-version-first-or-original-with-glosses)
